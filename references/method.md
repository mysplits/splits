# Training method

The method SPLITS coaches with. It works for triathlon and for single-sport endurance athletes.

## 1. Periodization

Count back from the A race and fill `plan.md` week by week.

| Phase | Typical length | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Base I | 3-6 weeks | Aerobic volume, technique, frequency. Almost all easy |
| Base II | 3-6 weeks | More volume, first threshold work (tempo, sweet spot) |
| Build | 4-8 weeks | Race-specific intensity, long sessions growing, bricks for triathletes |
| Peak | 2-3 weeks | Race simulation, the hardest specific sessions |
| Taper | 8-14 days | Volume drops, intensity and frequency stay |
| Post-race | 1-3 weeks | Recovery, then the next cycle |

Compress or stretch the phases to fit the time available. With less than 8 weeks to the race, skip Base I and say so.

### Load and recovery: 3:1

- **Never more than 3 load weeks in a row.** The 4th is a recovery week: volume down 30-40%, **same frequency**, a little intensity kept so the body does not switch off.
- A trip, an illness or a heavy work week counts as a recovery week. Move the cycle instead of stacking a 4th load week after it.
- **After any break of a week or more, the first week back is a ramp-up at 60-70% of the previous volume.**
- Each load week goes up about 5-10% in volume over the previous one, never more than 10%.
- The week type is written in `plan.md`. **Read it, never work it out in your head.**

### Taper

Based on Bosquet et al. (2007), the most cited meta-analysis on tapering:

- **8-14 days.** Shorter for shorter races and lower-volume athletes; longer for long-distance races and high-volume athletes.
- **Volume drops 41-60%**, progressively (exponential, not a single step).
- **Intensity and frequency do not change.** Short bouts at race pace and above keep the engine sharp.
- Typical shape: week 1 around -40%, race week around -60% with only short activations.
- **Count days, not weeks,** from the last real load session. The general race simulation goes about 2 weeks before the race.
- In the taper the athlete always feels they could do more. That is the point: tell them in advance.

### After the race

- No running for the first 7 days after a half or full distance race; easy swimming and spinning are fine from day 2-3.
- About 2 weeks of recovery after a 70.3 or a marathon, longer after a full distance.
- Extend recovery if resting heart rate stays more than 5 bpm above normal, sleep is poor or motivation is low.

## 2. Principles

### Polarized 80/20
- About 80% of the training time easy (zones 1-2, conversational), about 20% genuinely hard.
- Avoid the grey zone (zone 3) except in specific tempo sessions.
- Easy days are easy. Most amateur athletes run their easy days too hard and their hard days too soft.

### Train the limiter
- Find the discipline or ability that costs the most in the race (for most triathletes it is the bike: the longest segment and the easiest place to gain minutes). Give it priority in volume and quality.
- For each sport, know which lever moves progress for this athlete: distance (aerobic durability) or speed (threshold and VO2).

### Bricks (triathletes)
- At least 1 brick per week from Build on.
- The run off the bike starts short (15 min) and grows toward 30-40 min at race pace, or becomes the race simulation in Peak.
- The bike of a brick the day after a long ride uses the low part of zone 2: its job is to leave the run clean.

### Fatigue
- Quality days spread out: for example one run quality, one bike quality and the long or brick session at the weekend.
- Never three hard days in a row on running legs.
- **Before cutting a session for fatigue, check heart rate at a fixed power or pace.** If it is normal, the legs are tired but the system is fine.

### Health
- Hydration before, during and after long sessions. Electrolytes in the heat.
- No fasted sessions longer than 1 hour.
- In the heat: lower the intensity and increase fluids. Heat costs heart rate; let heart rate decide, not pace.
- Any health condition the athlete reports in `athlete.md` overrides the plan.

## 3. Weekly structure

Build it from the athlete's availability, not from a template. A reference for a triathlete with 8-12 hours:

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Swim (technique + aerobic) · rest or mobility |
| Tue | Run quality · strength |
| Wed | Bike quality (indoor) |
| Thu | Swim (threshold) · easy run |
| Fri | Rest or easy swim · mobility |
| Sat | Long ride (outdoor) |
| Sun | Brick or long run |

For a runner, a cyclist or a swimmer, keep the same logic: 1-2 quality sessions, 1 long session, the rest easy, 1 rest day.

### Strength and mobility
- 1-2 strength sessions per week in Base and Build, 1 light one in Peak, none in the last week of the taper.
- Strength goes after the endurance session on the same day, never before.
- In the taper: 2-3 sets of 8-10 at 60-65%, never to failure. The goal is to keep the stimulus, not to build.

## 4. Sessions per sport

### Swim
- **Always by distance.** Plain language: say "easy", "steady", "medium", "fast", not drill jargon the athlete may not know.
- Intensities as % of threshold pace (CSS):

| Level | % of CSS speed | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 80-83% | Warm-up, cool-down, recovery |
| Steady | 90-93% | Aerobic volume |
| Medium | 98-102% | Threshold sets |
| Fast | 108-112% | Short fast reps with long rests |

- An easy aerobic swim usually happens at CSS + 6-12 s/100 m. That is normal, not under-training.
- Equipment goes in brackets in the title right after its metres: `600 (paddles)`, `600 (paddles + pull buoy)`. They are different stimuli.
- **Short pools (for example 33.33 m):** only distances that are multiples of the pool, and times are not comparable with a 25 m pool (fewer walls, a few seconds slower per 100). Remind them to set the pool length on the watch.
- **Swim tests are not judged by heart rate.**

### Bike indoor (smart trainer)
- **By time**, intensity as % of FTP (ERG mode if available).
- Reference: recovery 45-50% · warm-up 50-55% · zone 2 60-70% · sweet spot 86-90% · threshold 95-105% · VO2 106-120%.
- This is where quality goes: power is controllable indoors.

### Bike outdoor
- **By distance.** Intensity as % of threshold power if the athlete has an outdoor power meter, or as % of threshold heart rate if not.
- Without a power meter, outdoor rides are **steady heart rate**: no speed or power intervals on the road (wind, hills and traffic make them uncontrollable). Quality goes indoors.
- Long rides in **discrete blocks with a floor and a ceiling**, on a flat known route, so the road does not decide the intensity.
- Power and heart rate do not translate 1:1 between indoor and outdoor. Use outdoor references from the athlete's own outdoor rides.

### Run
- Easy running guided by **heart rate** (a ceiling), quality guided by **pace** with heart rate as a secondary check. Ask the athlete's preference in setup.
- Runs planned in km go in distance; the run off the bike goes in time.
- A minimum session length that makes the trip worth it (around 45-60 min for most athletes).
- The cool-down after a hard block is where athletes fail: allow 1 min of walking before it starts.

### Brick
- Always two workouts: Bike + Run, same day.
- Run off the bike: "straight from the bike, no sitting down".

## 5. Naming

Titles say what the session is, with numbers. Never generic names like "Intervals", "Long ride", "Zone 2", "Sweet spot", "Tempo".

| Sport | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Swim | Distance chain, equipment in brackets | `400 + 4x50 + 600 (paddles) + 8x100 + 300` |
| Bike indoor | Reps and watts | `3x10' 189-198W / 5' rec` |
| Bike outdoor | Km and HR (or watts) | `65 km HR 139-150` |
| Run | Km and pace or HR | `2 + 6 km at 4:45-4:50 + 2` · `8 km HR<145` |
| Run off the bike | Time and HR or pace | `20' HR<145` |
| Strength | Plain type | `Full body light` |
| Mobility | Plain type | `Mobility + Core` |

## 6. Session descriptions

- First line: what it is and the total (`Total 2,000 m. No equipment.`).
- Then the blocks, one per line, with their targets.
- Then **the why in capitals, briefly:** what the session is for in this phase and the one mistake to avoid ("IF IT COMES OUT AT 4:35 IT IS WRONG EVEN IF IT FEELS EASY").
- Practical notes last: route, equipment, nutrition, what to write down.
- Under 2,000 characters.
